Florence: Women Lead

Field notes from a week-long women's leadership conference with Gonzaga in Florence.

11 field notes

11.05.2026Field Note Pentimento and Lunch On the conference's first day I went back to a restaurant from an earlier visit for coccoli con stracchino. Florence holds layers of past versions of me, and I let them sit together.
18.05.2026Field Note Why I Was in Florence The series opener: a small conference that felt more like a retreat, held under Raphael's School of Athens. It previews themes of care, power, place, and tradition.
18.05.2026Field Note The History of Florence Is Full of Women… If You Know Where to Look Art historian Cristina Bibbiani's tour surfaced the women hidden in Florence's history. Walking home, I wondered what maps I've been missing in other cities.
19.05.2026Field Note Respect Is Not Silence Unattributed notes from a panel of accomplished women on voice, legitimacy, and leadership style. The relief was learning that weight I thought was mine alone sits on a lot of shoulders.
19.05.2026Field Note Lead Beautifully, Live Beautifully Gonzaga alum Sarah Kinsella's session on "those who can, must" and not confusing exhaustion with seriousness. Living well is part of how the work gets done, not a reward afterward.
20.05.2026Field Note A Blank Page About Care In Denise Hossom's care-ethics workshop, my page stayed nearly empty while everyone else's filled up. Receiving care, it turns out, is the harder part for me.
20.05.2026Field Note A Woman on a Bicycle A short prose poem, inspired by Carolotta Ferrari's story, about a mother held up by a network of women across the city. Written differently from the rest of the notes.
20.05.2026Field Note A Global City at Human Scale A slow, loud group dinner on the first night shows how trust needs unhurried time. Real influence starts with people who have time to know each other.
25.05.2026Field Note The Power of Patrons Repeat visits to the Accademia and David lead to the question of what wealth and patronage owe the future. Powerful people leave fingerprints, and some become wounds, some beauty, some both.
27.05.2026Field Note The Weight of Tradition A visit to Florence's leather school, shadowed by the recent death of Tommaso Melani. It contrasts inherited craft with Francesca Gori's bags, which make history vivid instead of embalming it.
27.05.2026Field Note What Is in a Bottle of Wine? I barely drink wine, but at Antinori nel Chianti Classico I was captivated by people explaining what they love. Inheritance, stewardship, and tradition versus innovation in a centuries-old family business.
