March 2026

  1. 03.03.2026Timișoara The Third PlaceA coffee shop by the dorm became my daily office, where 8 a.m. counts as early and nobody asks you to leave after seven hours. How the café treats time became a way to read how the culture treats space and pace.
  2. 03.03.2026Timișoara Culture and Restaurants and LiverA practical primer on Romanian restaurant norms, starting with the fact that you seat yourself. A surprise plate of chicken liver pâté confirmed the universe's insistence that I eat liver.
  3. 03.03.2026Timișoara It’s not you, it’s the cultureAmerican small talk with cashiers and drivers gets baffled looks, because friendliness with strangers can read as intrusion here. My accent gives me away instantly, and daily corrections from servers became my language practice.
  4. 03.03.2026Timișoara The Coolest in the Parking LotI assumed "DUB (ENG)" on a movie listing meant dubbed in English and sat through 100 minutes of the animated film GOAT in un-subtitled Romanian. I still cried at the ending, and decided humility is sometimes the price of a ticket.
  5. 09.03.2026Hunedoara Corvin Castle and the Problem with DraculaCorvin Castle, with its Gothic towers and moat, looks more like Dracula's castle than Bran does. It sets the castle up as a place where stories live and opens the Hunedoara cluster.
  6. 09.03.2026Hunedoara Stories of Romania: The Well of the Three PrisonersA retold legend of three Ottoman prisoners promised freedom by John Hunyadi if they dug a well through solid rock. Whether the story is true is left open, but someone did dig that well.
  7. 09.03.2026Hunedoara Climbing Corvin CastleA comic account of uneven medieval staircases and what they do to modern calves. The view from the top made it worth it.
  8. 09.03.2026Hunedoara Stories of Romania: The Raven of Corvin CastleA folktale about King Sigismund, a village woman, a ring, and a raven, explaining John Hunyadi's origins and his raven coat of arms. It ends where Hunyadi's own story begins.
  9. 09.03.2026Hunedoara Chronicles of Romania: John Hunyadi and Matthias CorvinusA short history of Hunyadi's wins against the Ottomans and his son Matthias's reign as a reforming, book-collecting king. My favorite fact is Hunyadi's contemporary nickname, "Turk-buster."
  10. 10.03.2026Hunedoara Chronicles of Romania: Vlad III of WallachiaA sourced look at Vlad the Impaler as Western horror icon, Romanian defender, and one brutal ruler in a brutal century. It asks why the same violence gets remembered so differently.
  11. 11.03.2026Hunedoara The Rhythm of a Small CityA café-table sketch of Hunedoara in early spring, slower and quieter than Timișoara. Before tourist season the town still belongs to the people who live there.
  12. 13.03.2026Hunedoara Small Museum, Big PastA visit to Hunedoara's small Communism Museum, past factory gates 20,000 workers once crossed daily. Objects from the years I was a kid watching She-Ra made this history feel closer than the castle's.
  13. 14.03.2026Hunedoara Paper, Ink and IdentityThe museum's room of banknotes seemed out of place until I started reading money as a country's self-portrait. Mostly a captioned photo essay.
  14. 14.03.2026Hunedoara Tiny Worlds in HunedoaraA miniature train museum I found while video-calling Jason, who loves model trains. It turned into a reflection on hobbies as part of a life's story and a way to remember someone.
  15. 14.03.2026Hunedoara Following the BellsI followed church bells into an Orthodox service already underway and stood at the back trying not to intrude. A few minutes of chant and incense felt like stepping into another world.
  16. 14.03.2026Hunedoara Reflection: HunedoaraA wrap-up arguing the castle is only one layer of the town, alongside communist-era ruins, the promenade, and small museums. Staying put let the town become more than its headline attraction.
  17. 14.03.2026Timișoara MărțișorRed-and-white charms appeared all over the city for March 1, and I learned the tradition behind them. The image I kept is an older man carefully pinning one to his partner's coat.
  18. 14.03.2026Timișoara Stories of Romania: Baba DochiaOverlapping versions of the Baba Dochia and Dragobete legends tied to the arrival of spring. It closes with a list of Romanian idioms and their literal translations.
  19. 14.03.2026Timișoara Women’s DayFlower stalls took over every corner around March 8 as people bought tulips for the women in their lives. Like Mărțișor, a month built from small gestures.
  20. 21.03.2026București Sleeper Train to BucureştiMy overnight train trip started with a misread ticket, a wandering Uber, and a sprint to the station. The train had been delayed all day, so the urgency was real but the problem wasn't.

April 2026

  1. 05.04.2026București Romania and CommunismA history primer on how Romania came under communist rule and how ordinary people got by. It frames Romania as a country that absorbs change without disappearing.
  2. 05.04.2026București Bucharest’s Empty HousesWhy so many grand houses in "Little Paris" sit derelict, tracing it back to communist nationalization of private property. Through broken windows you can still see the fireplaces and crown moldings.
  3. 05.04.2026București Bucharest: Crisis Creates Opportunities… But for Who?The 1977 earthquake gave the regime cover to bulldoze neighborhoods, which made me rethink a phrase I have leaned on for years in crisis work. Crisis doesn't decide what gets built or erased, people do.
  4. 07.04.2026București Time for a spa day!Fourteen hours at a huge Bucharest thermal spa, saunas and red-light beds included. The highlight was a sauna ritual involving icy birch branches I did not knowingly sign up for.
  5. 08.04.2026Wien Vienna: On getting it wrongFor the first time I traveled without my usual color-coded Plan, arriving with one goal: see the white horses. Meet the planned version of me, who would have been efficient and missed things.
  6. 12.04.2026Wien Vienna: Dancing White Horses and ChandeliersWatching a Lipizzaner training session at the Spanish Riding School in the Hofburg. The horses' visible mistakes and years of repetition handed me a lesson I give others and rarely apply to myself.
  7. 12.04.2026Wien Vienna: A Kind of PentimentoOver coffee after the show, strangers told me how the Lipizzaners nearly disappeared in WWII. It connects to pentimento, the art term at the center of my leadership research.
  8. 13.04.2026Wien Stories of Vienna: Once Upon a WarA fairy-tale retelling of how the Third Reich gathered the Lipizzaners and Polish Arabians to breed a "master race" of horses, and how they were saved. It ends with Polish horsemen rebuilding their breed from a few dozen survivors.
  9. 13.04.2026Wien Vienna: A Wrong TurnHungry on Easter Sunday with no cash and a confused GPS, I ended up in a restaurant built on Roman ruins and had one of the best meals of my life. The planned version of me would have had a good trip and missed it.
  10. 16.04.2026Wien Vienna: Making FacesPartway through the Belvedere I stopped looking at the art and started watching how people react to it. Then I went back to the entrance and walked the whole museum again.
  11. 16.04.2026Wien Vienna: The Kiss and the CheckpointThe longest Vienna post: finally seeing Klimt's The Kiss in a crowd focused on getting the photo. It asks how museum design rewards documentation over encounter.
  12. 23.04.2026Wien Vienna: What the Glass TakesOn the Belvedere's quiet top floor, with less glass between me and the art, I thought about why famous paintings now sit behind protective glass after activist attacks. Something was lost, and I was lucky to see it before.
  13. 25.04.2026Kraków Krakow: UnexpectedFlying in instead of taking the train was a mistake, but the city surprised me. A trumpet call that keeps cutting off mid-note sets up a story for later.
  14. 25.04.2026Kraków Krakow: The Accidental Private Food TourA group food tour cancels and becomes a one-on-one with guide Jason, a Portland transplant who came to help Ukrainian refugees and stayed. It ends with more vodka than planned and a classical concert next door.
  15. 25.04.2026Kraków Stories of Krakow: The Dragon, The Bugler, The Enchanted KnightsThree Krakow legends: the Wawel dragon, the Saint Mary's bugler whose song breaks off, and knights turned into the pigeons of the market square. Told in the "bajka, nie bajka" (maybe true, maybe not) style.
